## Lesson 8.1: Why You're a Target (And How to Stop Being One)

**Type:** Instructional | **Duration:** 30 min | **XP:** 100 (Lesson) + 50 (Reflection)

### Opening Check-In

How are you feeling today? After bankruptcy, the world of borrowing can feel like a minefield. If you've already received sketchy-sounding offers in the mail — or felt tempted by one — you're not alone. That's actually why this module exists.

> **📌 You're Not Alone**
> Within weeks of a bankruptcy discharge, the average consumer receives 15–20 unsolicited credit offers. These aren't random. Your bankruptcy filing is public record, and certain lenders treat it like a dinner bell. Knowing this isn't meant to scare you — it's meant to prepare you. By the end of this module, you'll have a finely tuned radar for separating real opportunities from traps.

---

### Why Predatory Lenders Want YOU Specifically

Let's be direct: after bankruptcy, you are one of the most sought-after customers in the lending world. That might sound backwards, but it makes perfect sense from a predatory lender's perspective. Here's why:

**1. You have a fresh slate — and they know it.**

After a Chapter 7 discharge, you can't file again for eight years. After a Chapter 13 completion, it's two years before another Chapter 13 and four before a Chapter 7. Predatory lenders love this because it means you *can't escape* their loans through bankruptcy again anytime soon. To them, you're a "captive borrower."

**2. Your credit score is low — and they exploit that.**

A low credit score limits your options. When fewer legitimate lenders will work with you, predatory lenders step in as the "only game in town." They charge outrageous rates precisely *because* they know you feel like you have no other choice.

**3. You may feel urgency, shame, or desperation.**

Predatory lenders are experts at exploiting emotions. They know you might:
- Feel embarrassed about your credit history
- Desperately want to "prove" you're financially responsible again
- Need a car for work *right now*
- Want to rebuild credit as fast as possible

Every single one of those feelings is valid. But predatory lenders weaponize them.

**4. You want to rebuild — fast.**

The desire to rebuild quickly is one of your greatest strengths. But predatory lenders twist it into a vulnerability. They pitch their products as "credit builders" when many of them don't even report to credit bureaus, meaning they do absolutely nothing for your score while draining your wallet.

> 🐇 **Hariette says:** "When I got my discharge, I was SO ready to rebuild. I wanted a car, a credit card, everything — yesterday. That rush? That's exactly what predatory lenders are counting on."
>
> 🐢 **Sheldon says:** "And for me, after years of the repayment plan, I just wanted things to feel *normal* again. I almost signed up for a rent-to-own furniture deal that would have cost me triple. The finish line can make you drop your guard."

---

### The Psychology of Targeting

Predatory lenders don't just happen to find you. They use deliberate psychological tactics:

**Manufactured Urgency**
- "This rate is only available today!"
- "We can only hold this vehicle until 5 PM!"
- Countdown timers on websites
- Limited-time "approval windows"

*Why it works:* Urgency shuts down your analytical brain. When you feel rushed, you stop comparing options and start grabbing whatever's in front of you.

**Shame Exploitation**
- "With your credit history, this is the best you'll find."
- "Most lenders won't even talk to you."
- "You should be grateful for this opportunity."

*Why it works:* Shame makes you accept bad terms because you feel you don't deserve better. You absolutely do.

**The Savior Pitch**
- "We specialize in helping people just like you."
- "We believe in second chances."
- "Where others see risk, we see potential."

*Why it works:* It feels personal and caring. But a lender who truly wants to help you won't charge 25% interest to prove it.

**Complexity as Camouflage**
- Burying the real cost in pages of fine print
- Using confusing terminology
- Quoting monthly payments instead of total cost
- Separating fees across multiple documents

*Why it works:* If you can't easily understand what you're paying, you can't easily object to it.

---

### How to Recognize When You're Being Targeted

Here's your first line of defense — a mental checklist. If any of these are happening, your radar should activate:

| Signal | What It Looks Like |
|---|---|
| **Unsolicited contact** | Offers arrive by mail, phone, text, or email that you didn't ask for |
| **They found YOU** | You didn't seek them out — they sought you out based on your public bankruptcy record |
| **Emotional language** | The pitch focuses on how you *feel* rather than actual loan terms |
| **Vague terms up front** | They emphasize "low monthly payments" but won't clearly state the total cost, APR, or fees |
| **Rushed timeline** | Any pressure to sign today, now, before you leave |
| **Discouragement from shopping around** | "You won't find a better deal" or "Your credit limits your options" |
| **Too-good-to-be-true promises** | "Guaranteed approval regardless of credit!" |

## Lesson 8.2: Predatory Lenders — Know the Players

**Type:** Instructional + Quiz | **Duration:** 35 min | **XP:** 100 (Lesson) + 100 (Quiz, 10 questions × 10 XP)

### Opening Check-In

In the last lesson, we talked about *why* you're targeted. Now let's meet the specific players who do the targeting. Knowledge is power — once you know how each of these works, they lose their ability to catch you off guard.

> **📌 You're Not Alone**
> Millions of Americans use predatory financial products every year — not because they're foolish, but because these products are *designed* to look like the only option. Over 12 million Americans use payday loans annually. You're learning what most people never do: how to see through the marketing.

---

### Player #1: Payday Lenders

**How they work:**
You write a post-dated check (or authorize an electronic withdrawal) for the amount you want to borrow plus a fee. The lender gives you cash now and cashes your check on your next payday — typically in two weeks.

**Example:**
You borrow $400. The fee is $60. In two weeks, the lender collects $460 from your bank account.

**Why they're dangerous:**

- **The APR is staggering.** That $60 fee on a $400 loan for 14 days equals an APR (Annual Percentage Rate — the true yearly cost of borrowing, including fees) of approximately **391%**. A typical credit card is 18-25% APR. A typical personal loan is 6-36% APR.
- **The rollover trap.** When payday comes and you can't afford to lose $460, the lender offers to "roll over" the loan — extending it another two weeks for *another* $60 fee. Now you owe $520 on a $400 loan. This cycle repeats. The average payday borrower is in debt for **5 months** and pays more in fees than the original loan amount.
- **They rarely help your credit.** Most payday lenders don't report payments to credit bureaus. The loan does nothing to rebuild your score. But if you default, they WILL report that — or send it to collections.
- **They target location strategically.** Payday loan stores cluster in lower-income neighborhoods, near military bases, and in areas with higher bankruptcy rates.

**What they cost you:**
| Loan Amount | Fee per 2 weeks | If rolled over 5 times | Effective APR |
|---|---|---|---|
| $300 | $45 | $570 total cost ($270 in fees) | ~391% |
| $500 | $75 | $950 total cost ($450 in fees) | ~391% |

:::chapter-13
> 🐢 **Sheldon says:** "I spent years making payments through my Chapter 13 plan. The LAST thing I needed was to hand all that progress to a payday lender. If you need short-term cash, talk to a nonprofit credit counselor first. They know options you might not."
:::

---

### Player #2: Buy-Here, Pay-Here (BHPH) Auto Lots

**How they work:**
The dealership IS the lender. You pick a car from their lot, and they finance it directly — no bank or credit union involved. They often advertise "No Credit Check!" or "Everyone Approved!"

**Why they're dangerous:**

- **Sky-high interest rates.** BHPH loans commonly range from 18% to 29% APR — and some exceed that. A credit union auto loan for someone rebuilding credit might be 8-14%.
- **Inflated vehicle prices.** A car with a fair market value of $5,000 might be priced at $9,000 on a BHPH lot. You're overpaying from the start.
- **Often don't report to credit bureaus.** This is the cruelest part. They sell you on "rebuilding credit," but many BHPH lots never report your on-time payments. You get none of the credit-building benefit. (Always ask: "Do you report to all three credit bureaus?" Get it in writing.)
- **Aggressive repossession.** Many BHPH lots install GPS trackers and remotely disable vehicles if you're even a few days late. They *want* the car back — they'll resell it to the next buyer and keep everything you already paid.
- **Short loan terms, high payments.** They structure loans with payments that are hard to sustain, increasing the odds of default.

**What they cost you (comparison):**
| | Credit Union Auto Loan | BHPH Lot |
|---|---|---|
| Car value | $8,000 | $8,000 (marked up to $12,000) |
| Interest rate | 10% APR | 24% APR |
| Term | 48 months | 36 months |
| Monthly payment | ~$203 | ~$471 |
| **Total paid** | **$9,726** | **$16,955** |
| Reports to bureaus? | Yes | Maybe not |

---

### Player #3: Title Lenders

**How they work:**
You hand over the title to your car (which you own outright) as collateral for a short-term loan — usually 30 days. The loan amount is typically 25-50% of the car's value.

**Why they're dangerous:**

- **APR of 100-300%+.** Typical title loan: borrow $1,000, owe $1,250 in 30 days (a 25% monthly fee = ~300% APR).
- **You can lose your car.** If you can't repay, they take your vehicle — the thing you need to get to work, take your kids to school, and function in daily life.
- **Rollover trap (again).** Like payday loans, title loans encourage rolling over, piling fee on top of fee.
- **About 1 in 5 title loan borrowers loses their vehicle.**

---

### Player #4: Rent-to-Own Stores

**How they work:**
You "rent" furniture, electronics, or appliances with weekly or monthly payments. After a set number of payments, you own the item. No credit check required.

**Why they're dangerous:**

- **You pay 2-5x the retail price.** A $500 laptop might cost you $1,500-$2,500 through rent-to-own. A $400 washer might run $1,200+.
- **Miss a payment, lose everything.** If you stop paying, they repossess the item AND keep every payment you've made. You get nothing.
- **Doesn't build credit.** Payments typically aren't reported to credit bureaus.
- **Targets essential needs.** They know you need a refrigerator or a bed, and they exploit that need.

**Better alternative:** Buy used from Facebook Marketplace, Craigslist, or thrift stores. A $100 used washer works just as well as a $1,200 rent-to-own washer.

> 🐇 **Hariette says:** "I needed a laptop for my new job. The rent-to-own place wanted $35 a week for 78 weeks — that's $2,730 for a $600 laptop! I found the same model refurbished online for $380."
>
> 🐢 **Sheldon says:** "Patience pays. I saved $40 a week for six weeks and bought my washer outright from a secondhand store. No payments, no trap, and I still have the washer."

---

### Player #5: Subprime Mortgage Lenders

**How they work:**
They offer mortgages to borrowers with low credit scores — including post-bankruptcy consumers. This isn't inherently bad; responsible subprime lending exists. The danger is in the *terms.*

**Why some are dangerous:**

- **Balloon payments** (a payment structure where you make small payments for years, then one massive lump-sum payment at the end — often tens of thousands of dollars). If you can't make the balloon payment, you lose the house or must refinance on their terms.
- **Adjustable-rate mortgages (ARMs) with extreme adjustments.** The rate starts low ("teaser rate") then jumps dramatically. Your $900/month payment could become $1,500.
- **Prepayment penalties.** Fees for paying off the loan early — which traps you in the bad loan even if you qualify for a better one later.
- **Equity stripping.** They approve loans based on your home's value, not your income. If you can't actually afford the payments, they eventually take the house — and all the equity you built.

**Not all subprime mortgages are predatory.** FHA loans, for example, serve borrowers with lower credit scores at reasonable rates with government oversight. The key is understanding the terms.

---

### Summary: The Players at a Glance

| Type | Typical APR | Reports to Bureaus? | Biggest Danger |
|---|---|---|---|
| Payday lender | 300-600% | Rarely (only defaults) | Rollover debt spiral |
| Buy-here, pay-here | 18-29%+ | Often not | Overpaying + no credit benefit |
| Title lender | 100-300% | Rarely | Losing your vehicle |
| Rent-to-own | Effective 150-300%+ | Rarely | Paying 2-5x retail price |
| Predatory mortgage | Varies | Yes | Losing your home |

## Lesson 8.3: Red Flags and Deceptive Tactics

**Type:** Instructional + Interactive | **Duration:** 30 min | **XP:** 100 (Lesson) + 50 (Reflection)

### Opening Check-In

You now know the main types of predatory lenders. But here's the thing — predatory tactics aren't limited to payday loan stores and BHPH lots. They can show up in otherwise legitimate-looking loan offers from banks, online lenders, and mortgage companies. This lesson teaches you to spot the *tactics*, regardless of who's using them.

> **📌 You're Not Alone**
> Even financial professionals get fooled by sophisticated predatory tactics. The 2008 housing crisis happened partly because experienced investors couldn't see through the complexity. You're learning a skill that many "experts" lack. Give yourself credit for being here.

---

### The Red Flags: Your Early Warning System

Here are the 12 red flags that should trigger your defenses. Memorize them, print them, tape them to your fridge. Any one of these is cause for caution. Two or more together? Walk away.

---

**🚩 Red Flag #1: "Guaranteed Approval"**

**What it looks like:** "Everyone approved!" "No credit check needed!" "Guaranteed regardless of history!"

**Why it's a red flag:** Legitimate lenders assess risk. That's how they keep rates reasonable for everyone. If a lender doesn't care about your ability to repay, it's because they plan to profit whether you repay or not — through fees, repossession, or asset seizure.

**The truth:** No legitimate lender can guarantee approval without reviewing your financial situation. "Guaranteed approval" really means "guaranteed high cost."

---

**🚩 Red Flag #2: Loan Flipping**

**What it looks like:** Shortly after you take a loan, the lender contacts you to "refinance" into a "better" deal. You refinance, and they charge new origination fees, new closing costs, new processing fees.

**What's really happening:** Each refinance resets the fee clock. You're paying thousands in fees while the principal barely shrinks. This is called **loan flipping** — refinancing repeatedly to generate new fees each time.

**Example:** You take a $10,000 loan. After 6 months, the lender says, "Great news! You qualify for a lower rate!" You refinance. New origination fee: $500. New processing fee: $200. You've now paid $700 to save $15/month. It takes nearly 4 years to break even on that "deal" — and by then, they'll pitch another refinance.

---

**🚩 Red Flag #3: Equity Stripping**

**What it looks like:** A mortgage lender approves you for a loan based primarily on your home's value, not your income or ability to make monthly payments.

**What's really happening:** They're betting you'll fail. When you can't make payments, they foreclose and take the equity. This is called **equity stripping** — approving loans specifically designed to eventually transfer your home equity to the lender.

---

**🚩 Red Flag #4: Packing**

**What it looks like:** Hidden fees buried in the paperwork — credit insurance you didn't ask for, "document preparation fees," duplicate charges, "administrative fees," warranties you didn't request.

**What's really happening:** **Packing** means stuffing unwanted, undisclosed products and fees into your loan. It inflates the cost while you focus on the interest rate and monthly payment.

**How to catch it:** Compare the total loan amount on the final documents to the amount you actually agreed to borrow. If it's higher, ask why — and demand an itemized list of every fee.

**Cost benchmark:** Reasonable loan fees are approximately **1% of the loan amount**. If fees total **5% or more**, that's a major red flag.

---

**🚩 Red Flag #5: High-Pressure Tactics**

**What it looks like:**
- "This rate expires today"
- "I have three other buyers interested"
- "If you leave, I can't guarantee this deal will be here tomorrow"
- A salesperson who won't let you take documents home to review
- Being kept in an office for hours until you're exhausted

**The truth:** A good deal today will be a good deal tomorrow. Any lender who pressures you to sign immediately does not have your best interest in mind. Period.

---

**🚩 Red Flag #6: Blank Spaces on Forms**

**What it looks like:** Forms with blank lines for amounts, rates, or terms that the lender says "we'll fill in later."

**What's really happening:** After you sign, those blanks get filled with numbers that benefit the lender. This is sometimes illegal — but proving it happened is extremely difficult once your signature is on the document.

**Rule:** Never sign a document with blank spaces. Ever. For any reason.

---

**🚩 Red Flag #7: Income Falsification**

**What it looks like:** The lender tells you to overstate your income on the application, or does it themselves. "Just put down $55,000 — that'll get you approved."

**What's really happening:** They're getting you into a loan you can't afford. When you default, they benefit (through fees, repossession, or foreclosure). YOU bear the consequences, and stating false information on a loan application can also have legal consequences for you.

**Rule:** If a lender suggests you misrepresent your income, leave immediately. That is not a lender who wants you to succeed.

---

**🚩 Red Flag #8: Focusing on Monthly Payment, Not Total Cost**

**What it looks like:** "Just $199 a month!" "Affordable monthly payments!" The salesperson keeps steering conversation to the monthly number and away from the total cost, interest rate, or loan term.

**What's really happening:** $199/month for 84 months (7 years) = $16,716. For a car worth $8,000. The monthly payment is the least important number. The most important numbers are: total cost, APR, and loan term.

---

**🚩 Red Flag #9: Prepayment Penalties**

**What it looks like:** A clause in your loan agreement that charges you a fee if you pay off the loan early.

**What's really happening:** This locks you in. Even if your credit improves and you qualify for a much better rate elsewhere, you can't escape without paying a penalty — sometimes thousands of dollars. Prepayment penalties exist to prevent you from leaving a bad deal.

---

**🚩 Red Flag #10: "No Need to Read the Fine Print"**

**What it looks like:** "Oh, that's just standard language." "Don't worry about that section." "Everyone signs this; it's routine." Rushing you past the details.

**The truth:** If it's in the document, it's legally binding. There is no such thing as unimportant fine print. If a lender discourages you from reading, that's exactly why you need to read every word.

---

**🚩 Red Flag #11: Unsolicited Contact After Bankruptcy**

**What it looks like:** Offers arriving by mail, phone, email, or text shortly after your discharge — "Congratulations! You're pre-approved!"

**What's really happening:** They pulled your name from public bankruptcy records. They know you have a fresh start and limited options. This is targeted marketing to vulnerable consumers.

---

**🚩 Red Flag #12: Mandatory Arbitration with No Opt-Out**

**What it looks like:** A clause requiring all disputes to go through arbitration (a private process) instead of court, with no ability to opt out or join a class action.

**What's really happening:** This removes your most powerful legal protections. If the lender wrongs you (or thousands of people like you), you can't sue or join a class action. You're forced into a process that statistically favors the lender.

> 🐢 **Sheldon says:** "I keep a printed copy of these 12 red flags in my wallet. Before I sign ANYTHING, I pull it out and check every single one. It takes two minutes and has saved me thousands."
>
> 🐇 **Hariette says:** "And don't feel awkward about it! A good lender will RESPECT that you're being careful. If they act annoyed or impatient, that tells you everything you need to know."

## Lesson 8.4: Calculating the Real Cost of a Loan (APR)

**Type:** Interactive Exercise | **Duration:** 35 min | **XP:** 100 (Lesson) + 50 (Reflection)
**Badge Available:** "APR Calculator" (100% on all practice problems)

### Opening Check-In

We've referenced APR several times already. Now you're going to truly master it. After this lesson, no lender will be able to hide the real cost of a loan from you. This is one of the most empowering financial skills you can have.

> **📌 You're Not Alone**
> A 2023 survey found that fewer than 30% of American adults could correctly identify the APR on a loan when given the terms. By the end of this lesson, you'll be in the top tier of financial literacy. That's not an exaggeration — most people never learn this.

---

### What Is APR?

**APR** stands for **Annual Percentage Rate**. It represents the **true yearly cost of borrowing money**, expressed as a percentage. It includes not just the interest rate, but also mandatory fees, points, and other charges.

Think of it this way:
- **Interest rate** (also called the **nominal rate** — the "named" or "stated" rate) is like the sticker price on a car.
- **APR** is the "out-the-door" price — what you actually pay when everything is included.

**Why APR matters more than the interest rate:**

A loan with a 10% interest rate and $2,000 in fees can cost MORE than a loan with a 12% interest rate and $200 in fees. The interest rate alone doesn't tell you this. APR does.

---

### How APR Is Calculated (Simplified)

The exact APR formula is complex (it requires iterative solving), but here's the simplified version that gets you close enough to compare offers:

**Simplified APR Formula:**

```
APR ≈ ((Total Interest + Total Fees) ÷ Loan Amount) ÷ Number of Years × 100
```

Or, for short-term loans:

```
APR ≈ (Fee ÷ Loan Amount) × (365 ÷ Days in Loan Term) × 100
```

Let's walk through both.

> ⚠️ **Important Note:** This simplified method works well for **short-term, single-payment loans** (payday, title). For longer **installment loans** (auto, personal, mortgage), the actual interest is lower because you pay down principal each month — so the simple-interest approximation overstates the total interest. For precise APR on installment loans, use an online APR calculator (e.g., the CFPB's tool at consumerfinance.gov). **The point of this exercise is to understand the *concept* and spot major differences — not to calculate to the penny.**

---

### Walkthrough #1: A Personal Loan

**The offer:** $10,000 loan, 8% interest rate, 3-year term, $500 origination fee, $150 processing fee.

**Step 1: Calculate total interest paid.**
Using a simple interest approximation: $10,000 × 8% × 3 years = $2,400 in interest.

**Step 2: Add all fees.**
$500 + $150 = $650 in fees.

**Step 3: Calculate total cost of borrowing.**
$2,400 + $650 = $3,050.

**Step 4: Apply the formula.**
APR ≈ ($3,050 ÷ $10,000) ÷ 3 × 100 = **~10.2%**

**Takeaway:** The stated interest rate was 8%, but the true APR is about 10.2% once you include fees. That's a significant difference — especially on larger loans.

---

### Walkthrough #2: A Payday Loan

**The offer:** $400 loan, $60 fee, due in 14 days.

**Step 1: Identify the fee and loan amount.**
Fee: $60. Loan: $400.

**Step 2: Apply the short-term formula.**
APR ≈ ($60 ÷ $400) × (365 ÷ 14) × 100
APR ≈ 0.15 × 26.07 × 100
APR ≈ **391%**

**Takeaway:** That "$60 fee" is equivalent to a **391% annual interest rate**. Perspective: a typical credit card charges 18-25% APR. This payday loan costs more than 15x that.

:::

### Walkthrough #3: Comparing Two Auto Loans

Which is the better deal?

| | Loan A | Loan B |
|---|---|---|
| Loan amount | $12,000 | $12,000 |
| Interest rate | 12% | 9% |
| Fees | $200 | $1,800 |
| Term | 48 months | 48 months |

**Loan A:**
Using our simplified formula: Total interest ≈ $12,000 × 12% × 4 = $5,760
Total cost = $5,760 + $200 = $5,960
Simplified APR ≈ ($5,960 ÷ $12,000) ÷ 4 × 100 ≈ **12.4%**

**Loan B:**
Total interest ≈ $12,000 × 9% × 4 = $4,320
Total cost = $4,320 + $1,800 = $6,120
Simplified APR ≈ ($6,120 ÷ $12,000) ÷ 4 × 100 ≈ **12.75%**

> ⚠️ **Accuracy note:** Because these are **amortizing loans** (you pay down principal monthly), the actual total interest is lower than the simplified calculation shows — about $2,607 for Loan A and $2,188 for Loan B. The *real* APRs (calculated with an online tool) would be approximately 12.5% for Loan A and 10.8% for Loan B once you account for fees. The simplified method still correctly shows Loan B's fees make it the more expensive option — but for precise numbers on installment loans, always use an online APR calculator.

**Takeaway:** Loan B LOOKS cheaper with its lower interest rate (9% vs 12%), but the $1,800 in fees actually makes it the more expensive option. Without comparing true costs, you'd have chosen wrong.

>
> 🐇 **Hariette says:** "And the fee benchmark — 1% of the loan is reasonable. Loan B's fees are 15% of the loan amount. That's a red flag the size of a billboard!"

### The APR Cheat Sheet

| APR Range | What It Means | Common Products |
|---|---|---|
| 0-10% | Excellent | Prime mortgages, prime auto loans, 0% promo cards |
| 10-20% | Fair to High | Subprime auto loans, average credit cards |
| 20-36% | High | Subprime credit cards, some personal loans |
| 36-100% | Very High (often predatory) | Bad subprime products, some online lenders |
| 100%+ | Almost certainly predatory | Payday, title loans, rent-to-own |

> **Rule of thumb:** 36% APR is widely considered the threshold above which loans are predatory. Many consumer advocacy groups and some state laws use this as the dividing line.

##### Category C: Subtly Predatory (8 offers)

**C1: "Premier Financial — Personal Loan for Fresh Starts"**
- Surface: "We believe in second chances. Personal loans with competitive rates for post-bankruptcy consumers."
- Details: $5,000 loan at 29% APR. Origination fee: 6% ($300). Prepayment penalty: $200 in first 12 months. Reports to bureaus.
- Red flags hidden in details: 6% origination fee (above 5% red flag), prepayment penalty, 29% APR
- Correct sort: 🚫 Predatory
- Why: The marketing sounds compassionate, but 29% APR + 6% origination + prepayment penalty = predatory. The "savior pitch" is masking bad terms.

**C2: "AutoNation Certified — Special Finance"**
- Surface: Dealership offer with clean showroom, friendly staff. "We have special finance programs for all credit types."
- Details: 2020 sedan, fair price ($14,000). Rate: 18% APR. Term: 72 months. Total paid: $24,780. Includes $900 in mandatory dealer add-ons (paint protection, fabric guard). 
- Red flags hidden in details: $900 packing, 72-month term at 18% makes total cost extreme
- Correct sort: 🚫 Predatory
- Why: The car price is fair and the dealership looks legitimate. But 18% for 72 months with mandatory add-ons results in paying $10,780 more than the car's value.

**C3: "GreenPath Personal Loan"**
- Surface: "Online personal loan. APR from 5.99%!" 
- Details: The 5.99% is for excellent credit. For your credit profile: 34.99% APR. Origination fee: 4.5%. Fine print: rate can increase up to 5% if you miss one payment ("penalty APR" of 39.99%).
- Red flags hidden in details: Bait-and-switch rate, penalty APR, origination fee near red flag zone
- Correct sort: 🚫 Predatory
- Why: The advertised rate is unobtainable for post-bankruptcy borrowers. The actual rate (34.99%) plus penalty provisions make this dangerous.

**C4: "Home Solutions Mortgage — Low Down Payment!"**
- Surface: "Only 3% down! Get into your dream home sooner!"
- Details: Legitimate 3%-down mortgage through a real program, BUT this specific lender adds: 4.5% in origination fees, requires private mortgage insurance at above-market rates, and the loan has a soft prepayment penalty (you forfeit a "loyalty bonus" if you refinance within 5 years).
- Red flags hidden in details: 4.5% in fees (near red flag), above-market PMI, disguised prepayment penalty
- Correct sort: 🚫 Predatory
- Why: The program itself is legitimate, but this lender's specific terms are exploitative. The same program through a credit union would cost thousands less.

**C5: "Sunrise Credit Card — Pre-Approved!"**
- Surface: Official-looking letter. "You've been pre-approved for a $750 credit limit Visa card!"
- Details: Annual fee: $125 (charged immediately, reducing available credit to $625). Monthly maintenance fee: $8/month ($96/year). APR: 29.99%. Reports to one bureau only.
- Red flags hidden in details: Fees consume 29% of credit limit on day one, monthly maintenance fee, reports to only one bureau
- Correct sort: 🚫 Predatory
- Why: $221 in annual fees on a $750 credit limit. Before you buy anything, 29% of your credit is consumed by fees — which also hurts your utilization ratio.

**C6: "Consolidated Debt Solutions"**
- Surface: "Combine all your debts into one low monthly payment! We negotiate with creditors on your behalf!"
- Details: Company charges 15-25% of enrolled debt as fees. Instructs you to STOP paying creditors (damaging credit). Holds your payments in escrow while collecting fees. "Negotiation" may take 2-4 years. No guarantee of results.
- Red flags hidden in details: Extreme fees, instruction to stop paying creditors, no guaranteed results
- Correct sort: 🚫 Predatory
- Why: Legitimate debt management exists (through nonprofits at low cost). This is a for-profit debt settlement company that charges massive fees and damages your credit further. After bankruptcy, this is especially dangerous.

**C7: "FlexBuy Financing — 0% for 18 Months!"**
- Surface: Electronics store offer. "0% interest if paid in full within 18 months!"
- Details: Deferred interest (NOT waived). If any balance remains at month 18, retroactive 29.99% interest charged on FULL original purchase price from day one. Minimum payments set so low ($25/month on $1,200 purchase) that you'll still owe a balance at month 18 if you only pay the minimum.
- Red flags hidden in details: Deferred interest (not waived), minimum payments designed to leave a balance, retroactive interest
- Correct sort: 🚫 Predatory (or 🔍 Needs More Research — acceptable if player notes the deferred interest concern)
- Why: The offer is structured so that customers who pay only the minimum end up owing massive retroactive interest. You CAN use this offer safely — but only if you divide the total by 18 and pay that amount monthly.

**C8: "TrustBridge Auto — Certified Pre-Owned"**
- Surface: Professional website, good reviews. "Certified pre-owned vehicles with warranty. Financing available."
- Details: Car: $16,000 (fair price). Rate: 14.9% APR (typical for rebuilding credit). But: loan term is 84 months, and the "included warranty" actually costs $2,400 (added to loan balance). Total financed: $18,400 at 14.9% for 84 months = $30,408.
- Red flags hidden in details: 84-month term makes a fair rate very expensive, warranty packing ($2,400 added silently)
- Correct sort: 🚫 Predatory
- Why: The rate and car price are reasonable individually. The 84-month term and hidden warranty are what make it predatory. Total paid is nearly double the car's value.

##### Category D: Ambiguous / Needs More Research (7 offers)

**D1: "Regional Bank Auto Loan — Pre-Qualification"**
- Surface: "Check your rate with no impact to your credit score. Rates from 9.9%."
- Details: Soft pull pre-qualification. Rates range from 9.9% to 21.9% based on credit. Terms: 36-72 months. Reports to all three bureaus. Origination fee: $250 on any loan amount.
- Correct sort: 🔍 Needs More Research
- Why: Could be good or bad depending on the actual rate you're offered. 9.9% with a $250 fee on a $15,000 loan (1.7%) = reasonable. 21.9% with $250 fee on a $5,000 loan (5%) = red flag. Need to see your specific offer.

**D2: "Online Lender — Debt Consolidation"**
- Surface: "Consolidate and simplify. One payment. One rate. Apply now."
- Details: Rates: 7.99%-35.99% APR. Origination fee: 1-6%. Terms: 24-60 months. Reports to bureaus. Legitimate online lender with BBB accreditation.
- Correct sort: 🔍 Needs More Research
- Why: Wide rate range. Could be a great deal at 7.99% with 1% fee, or a bad deal at 35.99% with 6% fee. You need to see YOUR specific offer before deciding.

**D3: "Subprime Mortgage Broker"**
- Surface: "Specializing in non-traditional borrowers. Let us find the right mortgage for you."
- Details: Broker (not a direct lender). Will shop multiple lenders on your behalf. Broker fee: 1-2% of loan. States they can find rates from 6.5%-12% for post-bankruptcy borrowers. No prepayment penalty on loans they recommend.
- Correct sort: 🔍 Needs More Research
- Why: Mortgage brokers can be valuable — they shop around for you. But the 1-2% broker fee on top of lender fees needs scrutiny. Get the full fee breakdown before committing. Compare their best offer against what you find directly from credit unions.

**D4: "Store Credit Card — 10% Off First Purchase"**
- Surface: "Open a store card today and save 10% on your purchase!"
- Details: 26.99% APR. No annual fee. Reports to bureaus. $500 credit limit. 10% off one purchase (savings: $30-50 on a typical purchase).
- Correct sort: 🔍 Needs More Research
- Why: Not predatory per se — many store cards have high APRs. If you pay in full each month (never carrying a balance), the APR doesn't matter and you get the discount + credit building. But if you carry a balance, 26.99% is expensive. Depends on YOUR discipline.

**D5: "Peer-to-Peer Lending Platform"**
- Surface: "Borrow from real people, not banks. Rates from 6.99%."
- Details: Rates: 6.99%-35.99%. Origination fee: 1-5%. Terms: 36-60 months. Reports to bureaus. Platform is legitimate and regulated. Your specific rate depends on their proprietary scoring.
- Correct sort: 🔍 Needs More Research
- Why: Same as D2 — wide range of possible outcomes. Need to see your specific offer. The platform itself isn't predatory, but your terms might not be favorable.

**D6: "Buy Now, Pay Later (Afterpay)"**
- Surface: "Split any purchase into 4 interest-free payments!"
- Details: 4 payments over 6 weeks. 0% interest if payments are on time. Late fee: $8 per missed payment (capped at 25% of purchase). Does NOT report to credit bureaus (positive or negative). No credit check.
- Correct sort: 🔍 Needs More Research
- Why: Not predatory if used carefully (truly 0% interest with on-time payments). But: doesn't build credit, late fees can add up, and the ease of "splitting payments" can encourage overspending. Good for a specific planned purchase; risky as a habit.

**D7: "Credit Union Payday Alternative Loan (PAL)"**
- Surface: "Short-term loan up to $2,000. Better than payday!"
- Details: Federal credit union program. Borrow $200-$2,000. Rate: 28% APR (maximum allowed by NCUA). Term: 1-12 months. Application fee: $20 maximum. Reports to bureaus. Must be a credit union member for at least 1 month.
- Correct sort: ✅ Legitimate (also acceptable: 🔍 Needs More Research)
- Why: PALs are specifically designed as a safe alternative to payday loans. 28% APR is the federally capped maximum — far below payday's 300%+. Low fees, reports to bureaus, reasonable term. This is one of the best options for emergency short-term borrowing after bankruptcy.

### Your Financial Immune System Checklist

Before signing ANY financial agreement, run through this:

1. ☐ What is the APR (not just the interest rate)?
2. ☐ What are ALL the fees? Do they exceed 1% of the loan?
3. ☐ What is the TOTAL amount I will repay?
4. ☐ Is there a prepayment penalty?
5. ☐ Does it report to all three credit bureaus?
6. ☐ Did I seek out this lender, or did they find me?
7. ☐ Am I being pressured to sign today?
8. ☐ Have I compared at least 3 offers?
9. ☐ Are there any blank spaces on this form?
10. ☐ Have I read every page of the agreement?
