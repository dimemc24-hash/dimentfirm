### Why Wait 90 Days?

After your bankruptcy discharge, it takes the credit bureaus (the companies that track your credit history) a few weeks to update their records. Waiting about **90 days** gives them time to process everything so you're looking at an accurate picture.

**Credit bureau** — A company that collects information about your borrowing and payment history. There are three major ones: Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion.

**Discharge** — The court order that officially wipes out (eliminates) the debts included in your bankruptcy. This is the finish line of the bankruptcy process.

---

### How to Pull Your Free Credit Reports

You are entitled to **free credit reports** from all three bureaus every week through **AnnualCreditReport.com**. This is the only official site — don't use lookalike sites that try to charge you.

#### Step-by-Step:

1. Go to [AnnualCreditReport.com](https://www.annualcreditreport.com)
2. Click "Request your free credit reports"
3. Fill in your name, address, Social Security number, and date of birth
4. Select **all three bureaus** — Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion
5. Answer the security verification questions (they'll ask about past addresses, loans, etc.)
6. Download or print each report


> 🐇 **Hariette says:** "Pull all three! Each bureau might have different information. I once found an error on Experian that wasn't on the other two."
> 🐢 **Sheldon says:** "Take your time reading through each one. There's no rush. I like to print mine out and use a highlighter."

**Pro tip:** Save each report as a PDF on your computer so you have a record.

### What to Look For

Every credit report has the same basic sections:

| Section | What It Shows |
|---------|--------------|
| **Personal Information** | Your name, addresses, employer, Social Security number |
| **Accounts (Trade Lines)** | Every credit card, loan, and debt — with status and balance |
| **Public Records** | Your bankruptcy filing |
| **Inquiries** | Who has checked your credit recently |

#### Your Audit Checklist

For **every debt that was included in your bankruptcy**, verify:

- [ ] The account shows a **$0 balance**
- [ ] The status reads **"Discharged in bankruptcy"** or **"Included in bankruptcy"**
- [ ] The account is **not** listed as "open," "active," "past due," or "charged off" with a balance
- [ ] No incorrect late payments are showing *after* your bankruptcy filing date

For your **bankruptcy public record entry**, verify:

- [ ] It shows the correct filing date
- [ ] It shows the correct discharge date
- [ ] The chapter (7 or 13) is correct

### What Is a Credit Score?

A **credit score** is a three-digit number (ranging from 300 to 850) that predicts how likely you are to pay back borrowed money. That's it. It's not a measure of your worth, your intelligence, or your character. It's a prediction tool.

The most commonly used score is called a **FICO score**, created by the Fair Isaac Corporation. Lenders (banks, credit card companies, landlords) use it to decide whether to lend to you and at what interest rate.

**Interest rate** — The percentage a lender charges you for borrowing money. A higher credit score usually means a lower interest rate, which means you pay less over time.

---

### The FICO Breakdown: What Actually Matters

Your FICO score is calculated from five categories. Here's exactly how much each one counts:

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│           YOUR FICO SCORE RECIPE                │
│                                                  │
│  ████████████████████████████████████  35%  Payment History     │
│  ██████████████████████████████       30%  Credit Utilization  │
│  ███████████████                      15%  Length of History   │
│  ██████████                           10%  Credit Mix         │
│  ██████████                           10%  New Inquiries      │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

Let's break each one down:

#### 1. Payment History — 35% (The Big One)

This is the single most important factor. It tracks whether you pay your bills on time.

- Every on-time payment helps your score
- Even one late payment (30+ days) can drop your score significantly
- The more recent a late payment, the more it hurts
- After bankruptcy, you have a clean slate for new accounts — every on-time payment builds momentum


> 🐇 **Hariette says:** "This is the one thing you have TOTAL control over. Pay on time, every time. Set up autopay and forget about it!"
> 🐢 **Sheldon says:** "I set a phone reminder 5 days before every due date. Belt and suspenders — that's my style."

#### 2. Credit Utilization — 30% (The Quick Win)

**Credit utilization** is the percentage of your available credit that you're currently using.

**Formula:** (Total Balances ÷ Total Credit Limits) × 100 = Utilization %

Example: If your credit card has a $500 limit and you have a $50 balance, your utilization is 10%.

- **Under 30%** = Good
- **Under 10%** = Excellent
- **0%** is actually slightly worse than 1-9% (lenders want to see you use credit responsibly)

This is the fastest way to improve your score because it resets every month when your statement closes.

#### 3. Length of Credit History — 15%

This measures how long you've had credit accounts. Longer = better.

- Average age of all your accounts matters
- Your oldest account matters
- After bankruptcy, this is your weakest area — but it improves automatically with time
- This is why you should open your first rebuilding account ASAP and keep it open forever

#### 4. Credit Mix — 10%

Lenders like to see that you can handle different types of credit:

- **Revolving credit** = credit cards (balance goes up and down)
- **Installment credit** = loans with fixed monthly payments (car loans, credit builder loans)

Having both types helps, but don't open accounts you don't need just for the mix.

#### 5. New Credit Inquiries — 10%

Every time you apply for credit, the lender does a **hard inquiry** (also called a "hard pull") on your report. Too many in a short period can lower your score.

- Each hard inquiry may drop your score by 2-5 points
- The impact fades after about 12 months
- Inquiries fall off your report entirely after 2 years
- **Soft inquiries** (checking your own score, pre-approval checks) do NOT affect your score

---

### 🔥 Myth Busters

| Myth | Truth |
|------|-------|
| "Checking my own credit hurts my score" | **False.** Checking your own credit is a soft inquiry — it has zero effect. |
| "I need to carry a balance to build credit" | **False.** Pay in full every month. You build credit by *using* the card, not by paying interest. |
| "Closing old accounts helps my score" | **False.** Closing accounts can hurt by reducing your total available credit and shortening your history. |
| "All debt is bad for your score" | **False.** Responsibly managed debt (on-time payments, low utilization) *builds* your score. |
| "Bankruptcy means I can never get credit again" | **Absolutely false.** You can start rebuilding immediately, and many people reach 700+ within 2-3 years. |
| "Income affects my credit score" | **False.** Your income is not part of the FICO calculation at all. |
| "Paying off a collection removes it from my report" | **Usually false.** The collection may still show, just with a $0 balance. Some newer FICO models ignore paid collections. |
| "You only have one credit score" | **False.** You have dozens of credit scores from different models and bureaus. |

### What Is a Secured Credit Card?

A **secured credit card** works exactly like a regular credit card, with one key difference: you put down a **cash deposit** upfront, and that deposit becomes your credit limit.

- You deposit $200 → your credit limit is $200
- You use the card for small purchases → you pay the bill each month
- Your on-time payments get reported to all three credit bureaus
- After 7-12 months of responsible use, many issuers will upgrade you to a regular (unsecured) card and **return your deposit**

**Why it works:** It removes the risk for the lender (they have your deposit as collateral), which means they'll approve you even after bankruptcy. But to the credit bureaus, it looks and reports exactly like a regular credit card. Every on-time payment builds your score.


> 🐇 **Hariette says:** "Think of the deposit as a security blanket — for them, not you. You're building real credit history with training wheels on."
> 🐢 **Sheldon says:** "I started with a $200 deposit. One year later, I had a regular card with a $2,000 limit and my deposit back in my pocket."

---

### How to Use a Secured Card the Right Way

**The Golden Rules:**

1. **Use it for one small recurring purchase** — like a streaming subscription or gas fill-up
2. **Pay the full balance every month** — never carry a balance, never pay interest
3. **Keep utilization under 10%** — on a $200 limit, keep your balance under $20 when the statement closes
4. **Set up autopay for the full balance** — eliminate the risk of a missed payment
5. **Never use it for impulse purchases** — this is a credit-building tool, not a spending tool

### Credit Builder Loans: How They Work

A **credit builder loan** flips a normal loan on its head. Here's the difference:

| Normal Loan | Credit Builder Loan |
|-------------|-------------------|
| You get the money → make payments → pay it off | You make payments → money is saved → you get the money at the end |

Here's the step-by-step:

1. You apply for a credit builder loan (typically $300–$1,000)
2. The lender puts that money into a **certificate of deposit (CD)** or savings account — you can't touch it yet
3. You make fixed monthly payments (usually $25–$75/month) for 6–24 months
4. Each payment is reported to the credit bureaus as an on-time installment loan payment
5. When you finish all payments, the money (minus interest/fees) is released to you

**Certificate of deposit (CD)** — A type of savings account that holds your money for a set period. In a credit builder loan, the CD holds the loan amount as security.

**Why it's powerful:** You're building an installment loan payment history (which diversifies your credit mix beyond just credit cards) AND saving money at the same time.


> 🐇 **Hariette says:** "It's like a forced savings account that also builds your credit. Two birds, one stone! I love efficiency."

### Authorized User Strategy: Piggybacking on Good Credit

Being an **authorized user** means someone adds you to their existing credit card account. You get a card with your name on it, and that account's payment history shows up on YOUR credit report.

**How it builds your credit:**

- Their account's positive payment history appears on your report
- Their credit limit counts toward your total available credit (lowering your utilization)
- You may benefit from their account's age (boosting your length of history)

**The ideal account to be added to:**

- ✅ Owned by a trusted family member (parent, sibling, spouse)
- ✅ Has a long history (5+ years is great)
- ✅ Has perfect or near-perfect payment history
- ✅ Has a high credit limit
- ✅ Has low utilization

:::chapter-7
> 🐇 **Hariette says:** "I asked my dad to add me to his oldest card — the one he's had for 15 years with a perfect record. His good history showed up on my report within a month!"

:::chapter-13
> 🐢 **Sheldon says:** "My sister added me to her account. We agreed I wouldn't even use the physical card — it went straight into a drawer. The credit boost was the whole point."

---

### Important Rules for Authorized Users

**For the person being added (you):**
- You do NOT need to use the card — you can cut it up or leave it in a drawer
- You get the credit benefit regardless of whether you make purchases
- If the primary cardholder misses a payment, it can hurt YOUR score too
- You can be removed at any time by the primary cardholder

**For the person adding you (your family member):**
- They are responsible for all charges — even ones you make
- Their credit is NOT affected by adding you
- They should only add someone they trust completely

**The conversation to have:**

> "I'm working on rebuilding my credit after bankruptcy. Would you be willing to add me as an authorized user on one of your credit cards? I wouldn't use the card at all — I just need the payment history to show on my report. It won't affect your credit or cost you anything."

---

### The Rebuilding Stack: Putting It All Together

The fastest way to rebuild credit combines multiple strategies:

| Month | Action | Credit Impact |
|-------|--------|---------------|
| **Month 1** | Open 1 secured credit card | Starts payment history + utilization |
| **Month 1-2** | Ask family member about authorized user | Adds history + limit + length |
| **Month 2-3** | Start a credit builder loan | Adds installment loan to mix |
| **Month 6-12** | All three accounts reporting on-time payments | Score climbing steadily |
| **Month 12+** | Secured card upgrades to unsecured; credit builder loan pays out | Deposit returned + savings released |

You don't need to do all three. Even one secured card with perfect payments will rebuild your credit. But combining strategies can accelerate the process significantly.

### Why Disputes Matter

Studies show that **1 in 4 credit reports contain errors** that could affect your score. After bankruptcy, the most common errors are:

- Discharged debts still showing a balance
- Discharged debts listed as "charged off" instead of "discharged in bankruptcy"
- Late payments reported after your bankruptcy filing date
- Accounts you didn't include in bankruptcy listed as included
- Incorrect bankruptcy chapter, filing date, or discharge date

Every error that makes your report look worse than it should be is **suppressing your score**. Fixing these errors is free, it's your legal right, and it can produce an immediate score improvement.


> 🐇 **Hariette says:** "Think of dispute letters as taking back what's yours. Your report should be accurate, and you have every right to demand it."
> 🐢 **Sheldon says:** "This is a methodical process — exactly my style. Follow the steps, send the letter, wait for results."

### The Dispute Timeline

| Step | When | What Happens |
|------|------|--------------|
| **You send the letter** | Day 0 | Send via certified mail with return receipt requested |
| **Bureau receives it** | Day 3-7 | Your return receipt comes back as proof |
| **Bureau investigates** | Day 7-30 | They contact the creditor to verify your claim |
| **Bureau responds** | Day 30-45 | They must respond within 30 days (sometimes 45 if you sent additional info) |
| **You receive results** | Day 35-50 | You get a letter with the results + a free updated credit report |

**If the dispute is successful:** The error is corrected and your updated report reflects the change. Score impact can be immediate.

**If the dispute is denied:** You can:
- Submit additional documentation and dispute again
- File a complaint with the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) at consumerfinance.gov
- Add a 100-word consumer statement to your report explaining the dispute
- Consult with a consumer rights attorney (many offer free consultations)

### Quick Refresher: What Is Credit Utilization?

**Credit utilization** = the percentage of your available credit that you're using.

**The formula:**

```
Utilization = (Credit Card Balance ÷ Credit Limit) × 100
```

**The targets:**
- 🟢 Under 10% = Excellent
- 🟡 Under 30% = Good
- 🔴 Over 30% = Hurting your score

**Key insight:** Utilization is calculated based on your **statement balance** — the balance on your account when your monthly statement is generated. If you pay down your balance BEFORE your statement date, you can control your reported utilization.


> 🐇 **Hariette says:** "Utilization is the fastest lever you can pull! It resets every month — so even if it's high this month, you can fix it by next month."
> 🐢 **Sheldon says:** "I keep a simple spreadsheet. My limit, my balance, my percentage. Let's practice that now."

### Exercise 3: The Multi-Card Calculation

If you have more than one card, utilization is calculated BOTH per-card AND overall:

**Scenario:** You have two credit cards:
- Card A: $500 limit, $40 balance
- Card B: $300 limit, $90 balance

**Per-card utilization:**
- Card A: $40 ÷ $500 = **8%** 🟢
- Card B: $90 ÷ $300 = **30%** 🟡

**Overall utilization:**
- Total balance: $40 + $90 = $130
- Total limits: $500 + $300 = $800
- Overall: $130 ÷ $800 = **16.25%** 🟡

**Important:** Both per-card AND overall utilization matter. Even though the overall is 16.25%, Card B at 30% is still dragging your score. Ideally, keep EACH card under 10%.

**Your fix:** Pay Card B down to $25 → Card B utilization drops to 8.3% 🟢, Overall drops to 8.1% 🟢

### Exercise 4: Real-Life Scenario

You have a secured credit card with a $200 limit. You need to buy:
- Groceries: $45
- Gas: $30
- Streaming subscription: $15

**Total spending this month: $90**

If you charge it all at once and wait for your statement:
- Utilization = $90 ÷ $200 = **45%** 🔴

**Strategy options to keep utilization under 10% ($20 max):**

**Option A: Pay before the statement date**
- Charge $45 in groceries → immediately pay $45 → balance = $0
- Charge $30 in gas → immediately pay $30 → balance = $0
- Charge $15 streaming → let statement generate → balance = $15
- Statement utilization: **7.5%** 🟢

**Option B: Make multiple payments throughout the month**
- Charge groceries + gas ($75) → pay $65 mid-month → balance = $10
- Streaming charges ($15) → statement generates → balance = $25
- Statement utilization: **12.5%** 🟡 (Still good!)

**Option C: Use the card for ONLY the streaming subscription**
- Charge $15 streaming only → pay in full at statement
- Statement utilization: **7.5%** 🟢
- Pay for groceries and gas with your debit card


> 🐇 **Hariette says:** "Option C is my favorite — simple, automatic, and perfect utilization every single month. Put one small subscription on the card, autopay it, done!"
> 🐢 **Sheldon says:** "I prefer Option A — I like using the card a bit more to earn cash back, but I always pay before the statement. Either strategy works beautifully."

### Core Mechanics

#### Score Engine

The score is calculated each round based on cumulative player actions, weighted by FICO factors:

```
Score = Base Score + Payment History Modifier + Utilization Modifier
        + History Length Modifier + Credit Mix Modifier + Inquiry Modifier
        + Time Decay Bonus (bankruptcy impact fades each month)
```

#### Monthly Time Decay Bonus
Each month, the bankruptcy's negative impact fades slightly:
- **Months 1-6:** +3 points/month (bankruptcy recency penalty diminishing)
- **Months 7-12:** +2 points/month

This means even with NO actions, a player would gain ~30 points over 12 months purely from time. But active rebuilding dramatically accelerates growth.

---

### Round Structure

Each round (month), the player:

1. **Receives a status update** — current score, accounts, balances, upcoming events
2. **Makes 2-3 decisions** from a menu of available actions
3. **Encounters a random event** (50% chance per round)
4. **Sees the score impact** — point-by-point breakdown of what changed and why
