### What Is an Emergency Fund?

An **emergency fund** is money set aside specifically for unexpected, urgent expenses – and *nothing else.* It sits in a separate account, untouched, until a genuine emergency hits.

Think of it as a financial fire extinguisher. You hope you never need it. But when a fire starts, you'll be incredibly glad it's there.

---

### Why This Is the #1 Priority After Bankruptcy

Here's the uncomfortable truth: **without an emergency fund, any unexpected expense can push you right back toward debt.** A medical bill, a car repair, a job loss – without savings, the only options are borrowing, credit cards, or desperation.

An emergency fund is your **barrier against relapse.** It's the single most important thing you can build right now.

### The Two-Phase Approach

We're going to build your emergency fund in two phases:

- Purpose: Survive job loss, major medical events, or other extended crises

## Lesson 5.2: True Emergencies vs. Slush Funds

**Type:** Interactive Exercise | **Time:** 30 min | **XP:** 100

### The Problem with Blurry Boundaries

The #1 reason emergency funds fail isn't that people don't save – it's that they **spend the money on non-emergencies.** Without clear rules, every want starts to feel urgent.

A **slush fund** is money set aside with no specific purpose – it gets spent on whatever feels important in the moment. An **emergency fund** has strict criteria for when it can be touched.

---

>
> 🐢 **Sheldon says:** "My rule became simple: if I have time to shop around, it's not an emergency. Real emergencies don't give you comparison-shopping time."

---

### 👬 "You're Not Alone" Sidebar

> *"I drained my first emergency fund in two months – on things that felt urgent at the time but definitely weren't. Setting clear rules for what counts as an emergency was a game-changer." – Course participant*

---

### The Liquidation Criteria

**Liquidation criteria** = the specific rules that define when you're allowed to withdraw from your emergency fund.

Your emergency fund should ONLY be used for:

#### 1. 🏢 Job Loss / Income Disruption
- You've been laid off, fired, or your hours were significantly cut
- Your business lost a major client and income dropped
- NOT: "I'm bored with my job and want to quit"

#### 2. 🏥 Medical Emergencies
- Emergency room visits, urgent medical procedures
- Necessary prescriptions you can't delay
- NOT: elective procedures, cosmetic treatments, wellness splurges

#### 3. 🏠 Essential Housing Issues
- Furnace dies in winter, roof leaks, plumbing emergency
- You face eviction and need to cover rent to stay housed
- NOT: wanting to redecorate, cosmetic upgrades, "nice to have" repairs

#### 4. 🚗 Urgent Transportation Repairs
- Your car breaks down and you need it for work
- Essential safety repairs (brakes, tires in dangerous condition)
- NOT: cosmetic damage, upgrades, "it's making a funny noise" (unless it's affecting safety)

#### The Quick Test

Before touching your emergency fund, ask these three questions:

1. **Is it unexpected?** (If you knew it was coming, it should have been budgeted.)
2. **Is it urgent?** (Does it need to be handled NOW, not next month?)
3. **Is it necessary?** (Would not addressing it cause serious harm to your health, housing, or income?)

**All three must be YES.** If any answer is no, it's not an emergency fund withdrawal.

**Type:** Instructional + Checklist | **Time:** 30 min | **XP:** 100

> C) I have a system that saves automatically
> D) I haven't started saving yet

If you picked A or B, this lesson will change your savings game. The secret? **Remove yourself from the equation.**

---

### Why Willpower Fails (And Systems Win)

Research consistently shows that people who automate their savings save **significantly more** than people who rely on manually transferring money. Why?

- Willpower is a limited resource – it gets depleted throughout the day
- "I'll save whatever's left" means savings compete with every other want
- Manual transfers require a decision each time – and decisions are tiring
- Automation makes saving the default, and spending requires the active choice

**The goal: make saving effortless and spending deliberate – not the other way around.**

---



> 🐢 **Sheldon says:** "I set up an automatic transfer on payday and honestly forgot about it. Three months later, I checked and had over $500. The best savings plan is one you don't have to think about."
>
> 🐇 **Hariette says:** "I'm the kind of person who will spend money if I see it sitting in my checking account. Automation moves it before I even know it's there. Out of sight, out of mind – in the best way."

### 👬 "You're Not Alone" Sidebar

> *"I tried to save manually for a year and managed maybe $200. Once I automated it, I hit $1,000 in 5 months. Same income. Same expenses. Just a different system." – Course participant*

---

### Automation Method 1: Internal Bank Transfers

**What:** Set up a recurring automatic transfer from your checking account to your savings account.

**How:**
1. Log into your bank's website or app
2. Find "Transfers" or "Automatic Transfers"
3. Set up a recurring transfer:
   - **From:** Checking account
   - **To:** Savings account (ideally a separate one – see Lesson 5.5)
   - **Amount:** Your planned savings amount from Lesson 5.3
   - **Frequency:** Match your pay schedule (weekly, biweekly, monthly)
   - **Start date:** Your next payday
4. Confirm and activate

**Pro tip:** Schedule the transfer for the same day as your paycheck deposit. The money moves before you can spend it.

---

### Automation Method 2: Split Direct Deposit

**What:** Ask your employer to split your paycheck – sending a portion directly to savings and the rest to checking.

**How:**
1. Contact your HR department or payroll administrator
2. Request a direct deposit split
3. Provide your savings account routing and account number
4. Specify the amount or percentage to go to savings
5. The rest goes to your checking account as usual

**Why this is powerful:** The money never touches your checking account. You literally never see it, so you never miss it.

---

### Automation Method 3: Round-Up Tools

**What:** Apps and bank features that round up every purchase to the nearest dollar and save the difference.

**Example:** You buy coffee for $3.75. The tool rounds up to $4.00 and moves $0.25 to savings.

**Popular options:**
- **Acorns** – Rounds up and invests the spare change (~$3-12/month fee)
- **Chime** – Free round-up feature with their checking account
- **Bank of America** – "Keep the Change" program
- **Many credit unions** offer similar features for free

**Reality check:** Round-ups alone won't build your emergency fund fast enough (typically $20-50/month). Use them as a *supplement* to automatic transfers, not a replacement.

---

### Automation Method 4: Savings Challenges as Automation

Pair these with your automated savings for extra boosts:

- **The $5 Bill Challenge:** Every time you receive a $5 bill in change, it goes to savings
- **The No-Spend Day Challenge:** For every no-spend day, transfer $5-10 to savings
- **The 52-Week Challenge:** Save $1 in week 1, $2 in week 2... $52 in week 52 (totals $1,378!)
- **The Weather Challenge:** Save the daily high temperature as cents ($0.72 on a 72° day)

### What If You Can't Automate?

Some situations make full automation difficult:
- Irregular income (gig work, tips, seasonal employment)
- No direct deposit available
- Bank doesn't offer automatic transfers

**Alternative:** Set a phone alarm for every payday. When it goes off, immediately transfer your savings amount. Make it the FIRST thing you do – before any other spending. This is "manual automation" – a habit so consistent it functions like a system.

---

### Key Takeaways

1. Automated savings outperform manual savings every time
2. Internal bank transfers are the easiest method – schedule for payday
3. Split direct deposit is the most powerful – you never see the money
4. Round-ups are a nice supplement but not enough on their own
5. If you can't automate, create a payday habit that functions like one

### The Three Rules for Emergency Fund Storage

Your emergency fund account needs to satisfy three criteria:

1. **Accessible** – You can get the money within 1-2 business days (it's for emergencies, after all)
2. **Separate** – It's not in your everyday spending account (to reduce temptation)
3. **Safe** – It's insured and not at risk of losing value

| FDIC insured | Yes (up to $250,000) |
| Minimum balance | Varies; some have fees below minimum |
| Separation from checking | Low – easy to transfer and spend |

**APY (Annual Percentage Yield)** = the total amount of interest your money earns in one year, expressed as a percentage. Higher is better.

**Best for:** People just getting started who need the simplest possible option.

---

#### Option 2: High-Yield Savings Account (HYSA)

**What it is:** A savings account – usually at an online bank – that pays significantly higher interest.

| Feature | Details |
|---|---|
| Interest rate | Typically 4.00% – 5.00%+ APY (as of 2025) |
| Accessibility | 1-2 business days for external transfer |
| FDIC insured | Yes (up to $250,000) |
| Minimum balance | Often $0 |
| Separation from checking | High – different bank = healthy friction |

**Popular options:**
- Marcus by Goldman Sachs
- Ally Bank
- Capital One 360
- Discover Online Savings
- Synchrony Bank

**Best for:** Most people. The higher interest rate is essentially free money, and the 1-2 day transfer time adds helpful friction.

---

#### Option 3: Money Market Account

**What it is:** A hybrid between checking and savings, often with limited check-writing ability.

| Feature | Details |
|---|---|
| Interest rate | Typically 3.50% – 5.00% APY |
| Accessibility | Same-day (may include debit card or checks) |
| FDIC insured | Yes (up to $250,000) |
| Minimum balance | Often $1,000 – $2,500 |
| Separation from checking | Moderate |

**Best for:** People who want higher interest with faster access. The minimum balance requirement makes this better for Phase 2.

---

#### Option 4: Cash at Home

| Feature | Details |
|---|---|
| Interest rate | 0% |
| Accessibility | Instant |
| FDIC insured | No |
| Theft/loss risk | High |
| Separation from checking | High (different location) |

**Best for:** A small portion ($100-200) for immediate-access emergencies (power outage, bank systems down). NOT for your full emergency fund.

---

### The Recommendation

For most people rebuilding after bankruptcy:

🥇 **Primary: High-Yield Savings Account** – Best interest, good separation, fully insured
🥈 **Backup: $100-200 cash at home** – For true immediate-access emergencies
🥉 **Starting point: Regular savings** – If you just need to START and will upgrade later

**The most important thing is that your emergency fund is SOMEWHERE separate from your spending money.** Don't let the search for the perfect account stop you from saving in any account.

### Interest: Why It Matters

Let's see the difference over time on a $1,000 emergency fund:

| Account Type | APY | Interest Earned (Year 1) | After 5 Years |
|---|---|---|---|
| Regular savings | 0.05% | $0.50 | $2.50 |
| High-yield savings | 4.50% | $45.00 | $246.18 |

That's $45 vs. $0.50 on the same money. Over 5 years, you earn nearly $250 in free money just by choosing the right account.

*(Note: interest rates change over time. These are illustrative examples.)*

### Key Takeaways

1. Your emergency fund needs to be accessible, separate, and safe
2. High-yield savings accounts offer the best combination for most people
3. Keep a small cash reserve ($100-200) at home for immediate emergencies
4. Don't let the search for the perfect account delay your saving
5. The interest difference between regular and high-yield accounts is significant

### 🎮 The Savings Sprint Mini-Game

#### Game Overview

You'll simulate 6 months (26 weeks) of building your emergency fund from $0 to $1,000. Each week, you'll make savings decisions while life throws curveballs. Your goal: reach $1,000 while making smart choices about what is and isn't a true emergency.

---

#### Setup

**Starting Balance:** $0 in emergency fund
**Weekly Savings Commitment:** $42 (automatic transfer each "week")
**Target:** $1,000 by end of Month 6

Each round represents one week. You'll see your automated savings deposit, then face a scenario.

---

#### Card Types

1. **👰 Savings Deposit** – Your automatic transfer hits. +$42 to emergency fund.
   *(This happens every round unless you pause it.)*

2. **🎯 Temptation Cards** – Something wants your money. Resist or give in?
   - "Amazing deal on a TV – 60% off! $200"
   - "Friends going on a weekend trip – $150"
   - "New phone release – your current phone works fine – $400"

3. **🚨 True Emergency Cards** – Life happens. Use the fund or find another way?
   - "Car battery died – $150 replacement"
   - "Unexpected medical copay – $75"
   - "Water heater broke – $300 repair"

4. **🎁 Windfall Cards** – Extra money comes your way!
   - "Tax refund: $300"
   - "Birthday money from family: $50"
   - "Sold old furniture: $80"

5. **⚡ Challenge Cards** – Test your knowledge
   - "Is this a true emergency?" – Classify correctly for bonus points
   - "Quick quiz: Where should you keep your emergency fund?"

**Example – Week 3:**

> - A) Withdraw $80 from emergency fund for the sale
> - B) Skip the sale – your emergency fund is not for shopping
> - C) Buy one item ($20) from your regular budget instead

> **Best answer: B** – Sales are not emergencies. Your fund stays intact. (+15 points for resisting)
